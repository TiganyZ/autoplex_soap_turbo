# Nuclear quantum effects on our water GAP, via i-PI + PIGLET

> Regenerated by [`../regenerate.sh`](../regenerate.sh) into `out/ipi_piglet/`; paths below are relative to that directory.

turboGAP driven by i-PI (`turbogap ipi`, turboGAP `feature/ipi-socket`). i-PI
owns the ring-polymer beads, the PIGLET thermostat and the integrator; turboGAP
owns the potential. Run and reproduction instructions:
[`workflows/water_ipi_piglet/`](../../../workflows/water_ipi_piglet/).

> ## RETRACTED: the PIGLET numbers below are not a band shift
>
> Plotting the spectra (`plot.gp`) showed what the band metrics did not: the
> PIGLET curve has **no OH stretch band at all**. It is a low, structureless
> plateau across 1800-4400 cm^-1, and the "centroid 3282" reported below is
> the intensity-weighted centre of that plateau, not the position of a peak.
> The -258 cm^-1 "shift" is therefore not a measurement of a nuclear quantum
> effect.
>
> **The cause is the thermostat, and it is in i-PI's own documentation.**
> `nm_gle` "attaches a coloured noise langevin thermostat to the normal mode
> representation of the momenta *and a langevin thermostat to the centroid*".
> Reading the deck's A matrix confirms it: the centroid's friction block has
> max|A| = 1.80 against 0.095 for every internal mode -- the centroid is
> damped nineteen times harder than the modes PIGLET exists to thermostat.
> That is by design. PIGLET is built to converge quantum STATISTICS quickly,
> and it does so by thermostatting everything, centroid included. An IR
> spectrum is a dipole time-correlation function, i.e. a DYNAMICAL quantity,
> and a damped centroid trajectory does not carry it.
>
> The Kcv diagnostic is unaffected and still says what it said: 7.40 -> 23.5 eV
> means the ring polymer really is spread, and the quantum STATISTICS of this
> run are good. It is only the dynamics that are unusable.
>
> This also explains the suspiciously close agreement recorded below between
> our GAP and q-TIP4P/F (3-7 cm^-1 on the quantum numbers). Both arms used
> PIGLET, both produced a plateau, and the centroid of a plateau over the same
> analysis window is similar regardless of the potential. It is a shared
> artefact, not two potentials agreeing.
>
> **What it needs instead:** TRPMD -- `<thermostat mode='pile_l'>` with the
> centroid friction made negligible (a large `tau`), so internal modes are
> thermostatted and the centroid is free -- and, for a defensible OH stretch,
> the dipole averaged over all 8 beads rather than evaluated on the centroid,
> whose effective potential is softer than the physical one and red-shifts a
> stiff mode on its own (the "curvature problem" of centroid MD).
>
> Everything below is kept as the record of what was run and what it produced.

**PRELIMINARY. 1000 frames per arm, one dipole model (`r119`).** The production
run is 2000 frames per arm with both dipole models; these numbers are an early
read taken to check the sign and rough size of the effect before committing to
the full sampling. 1000 frames at 2 fs with `ir_fft_acf_ratio = 0.2` is 83
cm^-1 resolution.

## The two arms differ only in the nuclei

Same potential, same starting structure, same cell, same 298 K, same 0.5 fs
step, same code, same machine. The control is a fresh run rather than the
earlier turboGAP-native classical trajectory precisely so that nothing but the
nuclei differs -- that one used a different integrator, thermostat and
timestep.

| | classical (1 bead) | PIGLET (8 beads) | shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| OH stretch centroid | 3541 | 3282 | **-258 cm^-1** |
| relative mismatch | 0.765 | 0.898 | +0.133 |
| libration share | 0.366 | 0.336 | -0.030 |
| OH stretch share | 0.287 | 0.150 | -0.137 |
| competing maxima | 1 | 1 | -- |

Downing's measurement is at 3368, so quantising the nuclei takes our GAP from
173 cm^-1 above experiment to 86 below it.

That the ring polymer is genuinely spread is not inferred from the spectrum:
the centroid-virial kinetic energy rises 7.40 -> 23.5 eV within 0.25 ps and
then sits flat, while the 1-bead arm's stays pinned at 7.3957 eV -- which is
exactly the analytic (3N/2) kB T for 64 waters at 298 K, so the contrast
between the columns is itself the check.

## Two potentials, one answer

Set the table above beside the q-TIP4P/F arms in
[`their_trajectory.md`](their_trajectory.md) -- an empirical force field, not
a machine-learned one, 648 atoms rather than 192, a different cell, run from a
different starting configuration:

| | our GAP | q-TIP4P/F | apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| classical centroid | 3541 | 3536 | 5 |
| PIGLET centroid | 3282 | 3285 | 3 |
| NQE shift | -258 | -251 | 7 |

Two independent potentials agree on the classical OH centroid to 5 cm^-1, on
the quantum one to 3, and on the size of the quantum shift to 7 -- and **both
overshoot experiment by the same ~85 cm^-1 once quantised.**

### What that most likely means, and what else it could mean

The reading it supports: the residual error is in the ESTIMATOR, not in either
potential. Two models this different would not land on top of each other if
the overshoot came from the potential, whereas the centroid approximation and
the PIGLET matrices are shared by both analyses -- and the centroid's effective
potential is softer than the physical one, which red-shifts an OH stretch by
exactly this mechanism (the "curvature problem" of centroid molecular
dynamics).

The competing reading, which these numbers cannot exclude: the centroid is an
intensity-weighted integral, and both arms above used the same `r119` dipole
model. If the intensity distribution across 2500-4400 cm^-1 is set more by the
charge response than by the vibrational density of states, close agreement
follows without saying anything about the potentials at all. The production run
carries the `tensoap` dipole model on the same frames, which separates them.

What argues against a purely dipole-driven coincidence is that the two
potentials do NOT agree elsewhere: libration share is 0.366 for our GAP against
0.548 for q-TIP4P/F, and the mismatches differ by ~0.08. It is specifically the
stretch centroid that coincides.

## Caveats that do not go away with more sampling

- **The centroid is not a dynamical estimator.** The dipole is evaluated on the
  ring-polymer centroid; the exact RPMD estimator averages over all 8 beads at
  8x the turboGAP cost. Read -258 cm^-1 as an upper bound on the shift.
- **PIGLET is a strong thermostat.** Correct quantum statistics, perturbed
  dynamics. A peak-position shift of this size survives that; a linewidth claim
  would not.
- **The mismatch gets worse, not better.** 0.765 -> 0.898. The band moves to a
  better place and the spectrum as a whole fits less well, mostly through the
  OH-stretch share halving. Reported because it happened.
