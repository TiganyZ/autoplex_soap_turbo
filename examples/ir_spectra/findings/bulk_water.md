# Bulk-water IR spectra: every arm, full range, against experiment

> Regenerated by [`../regenerate.sh`](../regenerate.sh) into `out/bulk_water/`; paths below are relative to that directory.

The complete spectra behind the dipole-model campaign — ten arms, the whole
computed range, the data as well as the pictures, and the scripts that made
them.

## What is here

    spectra/<traj>_<model>.dat     THE SPECTRA. 120 points, 0-4974 cm^-1.
                                   Columns: wavenumber, then intensity under
                                   each of the four quantum-correction
                                   prefactors (harmonic, classical, linear,
                                   none).
    spectra/experiment_downing.dat the measurement, on the model grid.

    data/bulk_water/turbogap/      turboGAP's own output for each run, kept
                                   so the recomputation can always be checked.
    data/bulk_water/dipoles/       the dipole time series each spectrum came
                                   from -- the actual primary data.
    figures/full_spectrum.png      the headline: whole range, both trajectories.
    figures/stretch_zoom.png       the OH band, where the question lives.
    figures/full_prefactors.png    the quantum arms under all four prefactors.
    band_metrics.json              every number the tables below print.
    scripts/bulk_water_*.py        spectra, metrics, figures. The estimator and
                                   comparison helpers are
                                   autoplex_soap_turbo.turbogap.ir_spectrum and
                                   ir_compare; the run and refit scripts are in
                                   provenance/.

Reproduce with:

    python scripts/bulk_water_spectra.py  # dipoles -> spectra, validated
    python scripts/bulk_water_metrics.py  # the tables
    python scripts/bulk_water_figures.py  # the figures

## The arms

Two trajectories, five dipole models. Both trajectories are **192-atom bulk
water** (64 molecules, 12.4294686575 A cube) from the **same energy potential**,
with **van der Waals on throughout** (`vdw_type = ts`, `vdw_rcut = 25.0`) in the
melt, the equilibration, the production MD and the prediction decks.

| model | training set |
|---|---|
| `old` | the baseline dipole model, 1228 classical frames |
| `new` | + 39 quantum-amplitude frames, flat sigma |
| `sigma` | the same 39, per-configuration `dipole_sigma` |
| `r79` | + round 2, 79 quantum frames |
| `r119` | + round 3, 119 quantum frames |

Frames are stripped to species+pos before prediction, so within a trajectory the
only thing that differs is the dipole model.

## A truncation bug worth knowing about

`spectrum.spectrum()` defaults to `max_freq_cm=4000`, while the prediction decks
ask for `ir_nu_max = 5000`. Left at the default, the recomputed grid stops at
**3971 cm^-1 — inside the OH stretch** — and the 10-bin gaussian smoothing then
behaves differently at that artificial boundary.

The spectra still looked plausible and the band shares barely moved, so nothing
announced itself. But the OH peak was wrong by up to two bins, and two arms had
their maximum pinned to the last grid point, which reads as a peak and is not
one. That silently produced the conclusion "the peak sits at 3971 for the old,
39- and 79-frame models alike, so the dipole model cannot move it" — when in
fact each of those models peaks somewhere different and the peak moves
monotonically.

`bulk_water_spectra.py` uses every deck's `ir_nu_max` (5000) and
checks every recomputed spectrum against the `ir_fft_spectrum.dat` turboGAP
wrote beside it. Agreement is **5e-10**; anything above 1e-9 and it refuses to
write. Do not remove that check.

**Resolution.** turboGAP's header reports the true resolution as **83.4 cm^-1**,
set by the longest lag kept (`ir_fft_acf_ratio = 0.2` of 1000 frames), while the
bin spacing is 41.8. A one-bin shift is below resolution; the shifts reported
below are two to four bins.

## The result, under w^2

| trajectory | model | mismatch | OH peak | stretch share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| classical | old | 0.656 | 3579 | 0.408 |
| classical | new | 0.660 | 3615 | 0.487 |
| classical | **sigma** | **0.646** | 3591 | **0.534** |
| classical | r79 | 0.661 | 3616 | 0.513 |
| classical | r119 | 0.650 | 3600 | 0.505 |
| quantum | old | 0.882 | 3992 | 0.313 |
| quantum | new | 0.814 | 3858 | 0.425 |
| quantum | sigma | 0.837 | 3900 | 0.458 |
| quantum | r79 | 0.808 | 3886 | 0.458 |
| quantum | **r119** | **0.777** | **3807** | **0.467** |
| **Downing** | experiment | — | **3411** | **0.689** |

**On the quantum thermostat every round improved every measure**, monotonically:
mismatch 0.882 -> 0.837 -> 0.808 -> 0.777, stretch share 0.313 -> 0.458 ->
0.458 -> 0.467, and the peak walked 3992 -> 3900 -> 3886 -> 3807, i.e. **185
cm^-1 toward the measurement**, from the dipole model alone.

**On classical MD nothing much moved** after the first batch: every model sits
between 0.646 and 0.661, and the 39-frame per-configuration-sigma fit is
marginally the best.

So the dipole model *can* move a band. Not by changing vibrational frequencies —
the dynamics never see it — but because IR intensity is frequency-resolved, so
reweighting modes within the band moves the maximum.

## What the spectra do not fix

Even the best arm peaks at 3807 against Downing's 3411: **396 cm^-1 too blue**,
and the stretch share is 0.467 against 0.689. That residue belongs to the
**energy potential**, which sets the frequencies. The salvaged frames carry
`REF_forces`, so refitting it is possible, but the reference is plain PBE with
no dispersion — awkward for a model that uses TS, and worth settling first.

## The other two diagnostics, for contrast

Neither predicted the spectral result, and one contradicted it:

| model | held-out dipole RMSE | dmu/dr along bond |
|---|---|---|
| baseline | 0.05675 | 0.5709 |
| 39 (sigma) | 0.07070 | 0.9064 |
| 79 | 0.07014 | 0.7876 |
| 119 | 0.07133 | 0.6047 |

RMSE saturated and the static one-bond derivative *decayed* — while the quantum
spectrum improved monotonically. **Neither is a sufficient proxy for spectral
quality**: RMSE is dominated by orientation, and the scan measures one bond in
one geometry rather than the distribution of environments the dynamics visits.
