# Our architecture, their data

> Regenerated by [`../regenerate.sh`](../regenerate.sh) into `out/bulk_water/`; paths below are relative to that directory.

Same model architecture, different training set and different DFT. The question
is whether our dipole model's remaining error comes from the architecture or
from the data it was trained on.

## What was trained

| | our models (`old` ... `r119`) | `tensoap` |
|---|---|---|
| architecture | soap_turbo `repo_s001` | **identical, unchanged** |
| `n_sparse` | 4500 per species | 4500 per species |
| sigma rule | per-config, 1.40% of \|mu\| | per-config, 1.40% of \|mu\| |
| training data | our clusters, 3-180 atoms | 800 bulk cells, 96 atoms |
| target | cluster dipole | **total cell polarisation** |
| DFT | FHI-aims DFPT, PBE, no dispersion | **Quantum Espresso PBE, SSSP, 55 Ry** |
| source | this campaign | Grisafi, Wilkins, Csanyi, Ceriotti, PRL **120** 036002 (2018) |

Data from `github.com/dilkins/TENSOAP` `example/water_bulk/coords_1000.xyz`,
byte-identical geometries to the Materials Cloud record `zkm7p-v9034`. The
`example/models/bulk-water/` directory in TENSOAP-FAST holds trained `.mdl`
files, NOT a dataset -- `mu-H2O.mdl` is their dipole model, and the training
data is the PRL's, published separately.

**Units.** Their `mu` is in atomic units (e.bohr), which the file does not
state. Established by regression: a unit-charge molecular sum correlates with
`mu` at r = 0.9799 with slope 0.9376 per Angstrom, implying an effective H
charge of +0.50 e and a molecular dipole of 2.83 D -- liquid water. Read as
e.A it would be 5.35 D, as Debye 1.11 D; neither is physical. Converted to e.A
here so every RMSE is on one scale.

**The polarisation branch is consistent.** Periodic polarisation is defined
only modulo a quantum; theirs is unwrapped, spanning +-1.1 quanta. An
inconsistent branch would have destroyed that r = 0.98.

## Caveat: their cells are too small for our cutoff

Cell edges 9.48-10.44 A (density 0.84-1.12 g/cm3), so the half-cell is
4.74-5.22 A and `rcut_hard = 5.5` exceeds it in **all 1000 frames**. Every
training environment therefore contains a second periodic image of neighbours
in the 4-5.5 A shell. Our IR trajectory is a 12.43 A box, half-cell 6.21 A, with
no such doubling -- a real train/eval descriptor mismatch. Shrinking the cutoff
would have removed it but would also have changed the architecture, which is
the one thing this comparison has to hold fixed.

## Held-out accuracy: much better, on an easier problem

| fit | test set | RMSE (e.A) | RMSE/std |
|---|---|---|---|
| our baseline | ours, 215 clusters 3-180 atoms | 0.05675 | 0.0756 |
| our r119 | ours | 0.07133 | 0.0950 |
| **tensoap** | theirs, 200 periodic 96-atom cells | 0.07539 | **0.0221** |

r = 0.9998. That is 3.4-4.3x better on the normalised measure -- but the sets
are not equally hard. Theirs is one system type at one size across a narrow
density band; ours spans 60x in system size and includes small clusters where
the dipole is a near-cancellation. The number says their fitting problem is
easier, not that their data makes a better dipole model.

## The spectrum, which is the actual test

`omega^2` weighting, 1000 frames, both trajectories, all arms validated against
turboGAP's own estimator to <= 5e-10.

| trajectory | dipole | mismatch | peak | libration | OH stretch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| classical | old | 0.656 | 3579 | 0.230 | 0.408 |
| classical | sigma | **0.646** | 3591 | 0.126 | **0.534** |
| classical | r119 | 0.650 | 3600 | 0.138 | 0.505 |
| classical | **tensoap** | **0.646** | 3590 | **0.248** | 0.438 |
| quantum | old | 0.882 | 3992 | 0.215 | 0.313 |
| quantum | **r119** | **0.777** | **3807** | 0.112 | **0.467** |
| quantum | tensoap | 0.823 | 3847 | 0.197 | 0.384 |
| Downing | exp | -- | 3411 | **0.209** | **0.689** |

**It does not win.** On the quantum thermostat -- the more physical arm, and
the one where the dipole model has been shown to matter -- r119 beats it on
every measure: mismatch 0.777 vs 0.823, peak 3807 vs 3847, stretch share 0.467
vs 0.384. On classical MD it ties `sigma` for best mismatch, but gets there
differently.

**What it does do better is the libration band.** 0.248 (classical) and 0.197
(quantum) against Downing's 0.209, where every model of ours sits at 0.11-0.23.
That is the one band where training on bulk periodic polarisation beats training
on cluster dipoles, and it is the band where intermolecular structure matters
most -- which is exactly what a periodic bulk target carries and a carved
cluster does not. It pays for it in the OH stretch (0.438 vs our 0.505-0.534),
the band that dominates the mismatch.

## What this settles

A dipole model trained on independent data, from a different DFT code, with a
**4x better normalised held-out RMSE**, is not better on the IR spectrum. That
is the strongest form yet of the Part 7 finding: held-out dipole RMSE does not
rank dipole models for spectra, and the failure is not a quirk of our training
set -- it survives changing the data, the DFT and the target definition.

The residual is therefore not the dipole data. It is the energy model, which
sets where the bands are: every quantum arm here puts the stretch at 3800-4000
against experiment's 3411, and no dipole model can move a band the dynamics do
not produce.
