# Both dipole models on the TENSOAP authors' own dynamics

> Regenerated by [`../regenerate.sh`](../regenerate.sh) into `out/their_trajectory/`; paths below are relative to that directory.

## Why this run exists

Every earlier comparison of the two dipole models put them on trajectories
generated with OUR energy model. That is fair between the dipole models but it
cannot separate them from the dynamics they were shown -- and our `r119`
training data was carved from trajectories made by that same energy model. A
third-party force field removes the confound.

## The trajectory: theirs, not ours

There is **no usable IR trajectory in either TENSOAP repository**. What exists:

| file | what it is | usable? |
|---|---|---|
| `TENSOAP-FAST/example/SAGPR-example-standard/frames.xyz` | 5 frames, 648 atoms | no |
| `TENSOAP-FAST/example/LODE-example-sockets/simulation.pos_0.xyz` | their q-TIP4P/F demo, 51 frames = **25 fs** | no (~1300 cm^-1) |
| `TENSOAP-FAST/example/SAGPR-example-committee/frames.xyz` | 1000 water **dimers** | no |
| `TENSOAP/example/water_bulk/coords_1000.xyz` | the training set | **no -- not a time series** |

The training set was checked rather than assumed: adjacent-frame displacement
is 0.983x that of random pairs and the lag-1 correlation of `mu` is +0.004. It
is decorrelated snapshots, so it carries no autocorrelation and no spectrum can
be formed from it.

What they DO ship is the recipe, in
`TENSOAP-FAST/example/SAGPR-example-sockets/input.xml`, and that is what was
reproduced here with i-PI's own Fortran driver (`-m qtip4pf`):

| | |
|---|---|
| system | 216 waters, 18.6445 A cube (their `init.pdb`) |
| potential | **q-TIP4P/F** -- an empirical force field, not DFT |
| ensemble | NVT, Langevin tau = 100 fs, 298 K, 1 bead |
| timestep | 0.5 fs |
| produced | 20 ps; first 2 ps dropped, 8 ps used |
| sampling | every 2 fs -> 4000 frames, Nyquist 8339 cm^-1 |
| resolution | lag 1.6 ps -> **20.8 cm^-1** (vs 83.4 in the 1000-frame runs) |

Both arms validated against turboGAP's own estimator to <= 5.0e-10.

**vdW is off in these decks, verified not assumed.** The dipole is a separate
GAP block that is a function of the SOAP descriptor alone, so vdW should not
touch it, and `vdw_rcut = 25 A` in an 18.6 A cell would sum over many images.
Checked on 20 frames with vdW on vs off: max |d mu| = 1.4e-9 e.A on dipoles up
to 16.2, i.e. relative 9e-11.

## Result

4000 frames, 20.8 cm^-1. **Under the quantum prefactor**, `harmonic` =
omega(1 - exp(-hbar omega / kT)), which is the standard correction for an IR
spectrum from a CLASSICAL dipole autocorrelation:

| arm | mismatch | ncomp | centroid | libration | HOH bend | assoc. | OH stretch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `r119` (our data, FHI-aims clusters) | **0.684** | 1 | 3536 | **0.346** | 0.042 | 0.023 | **0.493** |
| `tensoap` (their data, QE PBE bulk) | 0.810 | 1 | 3532 | 0.548 | 0.039 | 0.023 | 0.301 |
| **Downing** | -- | 1 | **3368** | **0.214** | **0.047** | 0.016 | **0.687** |

**Our data wins, by 0.126.** Both models put too much weight in the libration
and too little in the stretch, and theirs does so about twice as badly
(libration 0.548 against 0.214; stretch 0.301 against 0.687).

**Band positions are identical to 5 cm^-1** -- 3536 vs 3532, a quarter of one
resolution element. That is the expected control: position is set by the
dynamics, and both models saw the same frames. A dipole model moves intensity,
not frequencies.

## The prefactor decides the ranking, and that is the main lesson

Every prefactor turboGAP offers, same two arms, same frames:

| prefactor | `r119` | `tensoap` | theirs - ours | libration ours / theirs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **harmonic** (quantum) | **0.684** | 0.810 | **+0.126** | 0.346 / 0.548 |
| classical, omega^2 | 0.718 | 0.716 | -0.003 | 0.105 / 0.220 |
| linear, omega | 0.690 | 0.820 | +0.130 | 0.359 / 0.562 |
| none | 0.907 | 0.945 | +0.037 | 0.722 / 0.845 |

Under omega^2 the two models TIE and their libration share looks almost exact
(0.220 against 0.214). Under the quantum correction ours wins by 0.126 and
their libration overshoots by 2.6x. The prefactor reweights the stretch against
the libration by a factor of order omega, so it does not merely rescale the
mismatch -- **it changes which model is better.**

## A claim withdrawn

An earlier reading of the omega^2 column said `r119`'s advantage "disappears on
neutral dynamics", and inferred a matched-dynamics effect: that `r119` looked
good on our trajectories because its training configurations were carved from
trajectories made by the same energy model. **That was an artifact of the
omega^2 prefactor and is withdrawn.** Under the quantum correction the
advantage is present and roughly constant everywhere:

| trajectory | `r119` | `tensoap` | advantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| our GAP, classical MD | 0.747 | 0.852 | +0.105 |
| our GAP, quantum GLE | 0.780 | 0.868 | +0.088 |
| their q-TIP4P/F | 0.684 | 0.810 | +0.126 |

No vanishing, and if anything the margin is largest on the third-party
trajectory. The earlier `tensoap` libration result -- "essentially exact" --
was likewise an omega^2 artifact; under the quantum correction it is the worst
libration of any arm.

## The energy model is still confirmed as the residual

Same dipole model (`r119`), same analysis, same prefactor (harmonic), only the
dynamics changed:

| | on our GAP (quantum GLE) | on their q-TIP4P/F |
|---|---|---|
| mismatch | 0.780 | **0.684** |
| stretch centroid | 3773 | **3536** |
| competing maxima | 2 | **1** |

The band goes from a jagged multi-peaked blob to a single clean maximum and
closes 237 cm^-1 of the gap to Downing's 3368. An empirical water force field
beats our GAP energy model on band position, which is the strongest evidence
yet that the remaining error is the energy model and not the dipole model.

**Caveat:** q-TIP4P/F is parameterised for path-integral use, so its classical
stretch sits high too and its agreement here is partly fortuitous. The absolute
mismatch is not the point of this run; the difference between the two dipole
models on identical frames is.

## The PIMD arm: what nuclear quantum effects are actually worth here

> **RETRACTED, 2026-09-08: the -251 cm^-1 below is not a band shift.** The
> `piglet` deck uses i-PI's `nm_gle`, which by i-PI's own documentation
> thermostats the CENTROID as well as the internal modes -- and in these
> matrices nineteen times harder than the internal modes. PIGLET is built for
> quantum statistics, which it delivers; an IR spectrum is a dipole
> time-correlation function, and a damped centroid does not carry one. Plotted
> (`plot.gp`), the PIGLET spectra have no OH stretch band, only a low plateau,
> and the "centroid" below is the centre of that plateau. See
> [`ipi_piglet.md`](ipi_piglet.md) for the evidence and for
> what to run instead (TRPMD with a free centroid, and a bead-averaged dipole).
> The section is kept as the record of what was run.


Their shipped deck is one bead. q-TIP4P/F is parameterised **for** path
integrals, so run classically its OH stretch has to come out too blue, and the
168 cm^-1 by which it does could be blamed on that or on the dipole models.
The `piglet` arm settles it: same force field, same box, same starting
structure, same 0.5 fs and 298 K, eight beads with i-PI's own `nm_gle`
matrices. 12 ps produced, first 2 ps dropped, dipoles evaluated on the
**centroid** every 2 fs. Everything except the nuclei is held fixed.

Under the `harmonic` prefactor:

| | classical (1 bead) | PIGLET (8 beads) | Downing |
|---|---|---|---|
| r119 centroid | 3536 | **3285** | 3368 |
| tensoap centroid | 3532 | **3286** | 3368 |
| r119 mismatch | 0.684 | 0.845 | -- |
| tensoap mismatch | 0.810 | 0.894 | -- |
| r119 OH-stretch share | 0.493 | 0.250 | 0.687 |
| competing maxima | 1 | 3 | 1 |

**Quantising the nuclei moves the OH stretch down by 251 cm^-1** -- and that is
more than the 168 cm^-1 the classical arm was short by, so it does not correct
the error, it overshoots it by 83. The direction is what nuclear quantum
effects predict and the magnitude is the right order; the overshoot is not a
surprise either, and there are two known reasons for it that this run cannot
separate:

- **The centroid is not a dynamical estimator.** Dipoles are evaluated on the
  ring-polymer centroid, whose effective potential is softer than the physical
  one. Red-shifting an OH stretch is the textbook symptom -- the "curvature
  problem" of centroid molecular dynamics -- and it is a known artefact at
  300 K, not a property of the water.
- **PIGLET is a strong thermostat.** It gives correct quantum statistics and
  perturbs dynamics while doing it.

So read the 251 cm^-1 as an upper bound on the shift, not a measurement of it.

Two things do survive both caveats:

1. **Nuclear quantum effects are not a small correction to this comparison.**
   They are larger than the gap between our classical spectrum and experiment.
   Any claim that a dipole model is worth ~100 cm^-1 of band position has to be
   made on matched nuclei or not at all.
2. **The ranking of the two dipole models is unchanged.** Ours is ahead by
   +0.125 on classical nuclei and by +0.050 on quantum ones, and its libration
   share is closer to Downing in both. The advantage narrows but does not
   invert, which is the first evidence that it is a property of the model
   rather than of the dynamics it was shown.

By every other measure the PIMD arm is **worse**: the mismatch rises for both
models, the OH-stretch share halves, and the single clean maximum becomes
three. That is reported because it is what happened, not despite it.

## Reproducing

Everything needed is in `workflows/water_ir_tensoap/` -- decks, the prebuilt
i-PI driver, the starting structure and the run scripts. Nothing depends on a
session scratchpad.

```bash
cd workflows/water_ir_tensoap
bash build_driver.sh                         # i-PI's qtip4pf driver
bash run_md.sh classical                     # their shipped deck,  1 bead
bash run_md.sh piglet                        # the h2o-piglet deck, 8 beads

NFRAMES=4000 NRANK=4 bash analysis/predict.sh classical
NFRAMES=4000 NRANK=4 bash analysis/predict.sh piglet

bash examples/ir_spectra/regenerate.sh
```

The MD output and the dipole traces stay under `workflows/water_ir_tensoap/`
(`runs/`, ~300 MB per arm, and `runs_pred/`); only the analysis script, the
spectra and the figures are kept here.
